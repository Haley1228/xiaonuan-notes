Multimodal Sentiment Analysis (MSA) aims to capture multidimensional emotional characteristics by integrating heterogeneous information from textual, acoustic, and visual modalities [1]. Early MSA studies mainly focused on feature-level fusion strategies to learn multimodal representations [2], [3]. For example, the Tensor Fusion Network (TFN) [4] explicitly modeled high-order cross-modal interactions through outer-product operations over modality-specific features. To alleviate the computational burden caused by high-dimensional tensor representations, Low-rank Multimodal Fusion (LMF) adopted low-rank decomposition to achieve more efficient multimodal fusion.

With the rapid development of Transformer architectures, attention-based fusion methods gradually became dominant in MSA research due to their strong capability in modeling long-range dependencies and cross-modal interactions. Multimodal Transformer (MulT) employs directional pairwise cross-modal attention to capture interactions among multimodal sequences. MAG-BERT further injects acoustic and visual information into pretrained language representations through a gated adaptation mechanism, while MFN models cross-view dynamics with a memory-based framework. In addition, Self-MM enhances unimodal representation learning by introducing a self-supervised label generation strategy. Recent studies have also explored adaptive multimodal interaction mechanisms. For instance, KuDA leverages sentiment knowledge to dynamically weight different modalities, whereas Wu et al. developed a multi-loss fusion framework that incorporates contextual information to improve sentiment prediction performance.

To reduce the heterogeneity across different modalities, contrastive learning has been increasingly introduced into MSA. Fan et al. designed a multi-level contrastive learning strategy to strengthen intrinsic semantic consistency among modalities. HyCon further adopts hybrid contrastive learning to jointly model tri-modal representations, cross-modal interactions, and inter-class relationships. Meanwhile, MISA decomposes each modality into modality-invariant and modality-specific subspaces to enhance representation disentanglement, and ConFEDE further improves this framework through text-anchored contrastive learning.

Since textual representations are generally more informative and semantically discriminative than acoustic and visual features extracted by conventional tools, recent approaches increasingly employ text-guided multimodal fusion strategies. CENet enhances textual representations with asynchronous nonverbal cues, while ALMT utilizes hierarchical language features to filter noisy nonverbal information and construct complementary hyper-modality representations. DDSE further combines feature decoupling with text-centric progressive interaction. Specifically, it separates each modality into shared and private components while employing Mamba to preserve critical textual sentiment cues during multimodal fusion.
